Opening older files

What happens when you open a file made by an older version of MuseScore.

Updating old scores

When you open a score saved by an older version of MuseScore, FontDaw can offer to update how it looks. A box lists the improvements and you tick the ones you want, then press OK:

  • For older scores: a new notation font (Leland), a new text font (Edwin) and a new mapping of percussion notation to sound. All three are ticked at first.
  • For scores saved by MuseScore 3.6: the percussion mapping only. The appearance of the score may still change, because the defaults for beams, ties, slurs and spacing are different.

The box links to a video about the changes. Tick Don't ask again if you do not want to be asked for scores of that age. FontDaw applies your choices as one step, so you can undo it with Undo. Layout can change, so check the score afterwards.

Scores from very old versions are a special case. For a score saved before MuseScore 2.0, FontDaw shows a warning with the choices Cancel and Open anyway. The message says that you can convert such a score by opening and saving it in MuseScore 2.x first. If you choose Open anyway, FontDaw reads it without the usual version checks, and the result may not be right. A score saved by a development version of MuseScore 3.0 gets the same two choices.
