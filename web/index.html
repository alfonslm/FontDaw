FontDaw

The notation-first DAW

FontDaw is a music workstation built around the score.

You write music in standard notation, then record, arrange and mix audio around it in the same project.

Early development — nothing to download yet.

Already faster. FontDaw runs on a modified audio engine that MuseScore Studio does not have: when you export each part to its own audio file, all parts are rendered in parallel in a single pass. That makes part exports up to 5× faster: a 16-part score that took over five minutes now exports in about one.

Goals

What FontDaw is being built to do. Ticked items already work; the rest are planned.

  • Planned: The score stays at the centre. Full notation editing, parts and printing, as in MuseScore Studio.
    • Done: Notation editing, parts and printing
    • Done: MusicXML, MIDI and other score formats
    • Done: Score and audio always in sync, shown together in the score
  • Planned: Audio tracks next to the score. Import recordings and backing tracks and play them in sync with the notation.
    • Done: Import WAV files and play them in time with the score
    • Planned: Import FLAC, MP3 and Ogg files
    • Done: Waveform shown on an audio staff in the score
    • Planned: Audio staves in page view, in print and in PDFs
    • Planned: Playback locked to the score, following tempo changes
  • Planned: Recording. Record vocals and instruments straight into the project, in time with the music.
    • Done: Metronome and count-in
    • Planned: Audio input from your sound card, with input monitoring
    • Planned: Latency compensation, so takes land on the beat
    • Planned: Punch-in and loop recording with takes
  • Planned: An arrangement timeline. See score parts and audio tracks side by side.
    • Planned: Move, trim, split and fade clips
    • Planned: Clip gain, and snap to bars and beats
    • Planned: Recordings that follow the score's tempo at the same pitch, with beat markers you can edit
    • Planned: One undo history for score and audio edits
  • Planned: A piano roll. Shape how notes are played without changing how they are written.
    • Planned: Note timing and length as played
    • Planned: Velocity and controller lanes
    • Planned: Keep recorded MIDI performances, not only the quantised notes
  • Planned: Mixing. Effects, instruments, sends and automation.
    • Done: Volume, pan, mute, solo and aux sends
    • Done: VST3 effects and instruments
    • Planned: Plugin delay compensation
    • Planned: Automation lanes on the timeline
    • Planned: Buses and sidechains
    • Planned: VST instruments that follow the score's tempo and position, also through fermatas and caesuras
  • Planned: Export. A finished mix, or one file per track.
    • Done: Mixdown to WAV, FLAC, MP3 and Ogg
    • Done: One file per part, rendered in parallel: up to 5× faster
    • Planned: Audio tracks included in every export
    • Planned: Bit depth, dither and loudness options
    • Planned: MIDI export that keeps fermatas and caesuras as real pauses
  • Planned: Quick to work with. Commands where your hands already are.
    • Planned: A pointer menu and a ring menu at the mouse for quick commands
    • Planned: Shortcut layouts for one hand on the mouse and for both hands on the keyboard, next to MuseScore's own
  • Planned: Free and open. GPLv3, with no accounts and no proprietary sound library required.
    • Done: Free software under the GPLv3
    • Done: No accounts or online services needed
    • Planned: Open sound formats (SoundFont, SFZ) instead of a proprietary library
